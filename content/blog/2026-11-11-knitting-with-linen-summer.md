With summer settling in here in South Africa, wool can start to feel like a winter-only friend. That's when linen comes into its own. It's one of the oldest plant fibres around, and it makes knits that are light, cool and lovely to wear on a hot day.

Linen does behave quite differently from wool, though, so a few small changes make it far more fun to work with.

## What to expect on the needles

Linen has almost no stretch. There's none of the springy bounce you get from wool, and straight off the skein it can feel firm, even a little rough. Don't let that put you off. The magic happens later, in the wash.

A few things that help:

- **Choose wooden or bamboo needles.** Linen is slippery, and metal needles can make it slide about. Wood gives a bit of grip and helps keep your stitches steady.
- **Keep your hands relaxed.** Let the yarn run loosely through your fingers. Pulling tight won't make the fabric neater and can leave your hands sore.
- **Change needle size, not tension.** If your gauge is off, go up or down a size rather than knitting tighter or looser.
- **Wind it into a ball by hand** and work from the outside, as linen tends to tangle when pulled from the centre.

## Why the swatch really matters

Linen stitches often look a bit uneven while you knit. That's normal. Washing and blocking evens them out, and the fabric can change size and feel quite a lot in the process. Some linens even tighten slightly, which is the opposite of what wool usually does.

So knit a swatch, wash it the way you plan to wash the finished piece, let it dry, and only then measure. It's the most reliable way to know what you'll end up with.

## Projects that suit linen

Because it doesn't stretch, linen loves loose, flowing shapes: tees, tanks, easy cardigans, wraps and shawls. It's less happy as socks, mittens or snug hats, which need give to fit well. Patterns written for linen will usually work better than wool patterns with linen swapped in.

The reward is a fabric that softens with every wear and wash, hangs beautifully, and keeps its good looks for years.
