You've cast off, and then you turn your knitting over and find a little crowd of yarn tails waiting. Weaving in ends isn't the most exciting step, but done well it's the difference between a piece that lasts for years and one that slowly unravels at the edges.

The good news? It's a simple skill, and a few habits make it quick and almost invisible.

## Before you start

- **Join new yarn at the edge** where you can. Starting a new ball at the end of a row gives you a neat seam or selvedge to hide the tails in later.
- **Leave enough tail to work with.** A tail that's too short to thread on a needle is a frustrating thing.
- **Skip the knot.** Knots make a hard bump on the inside and can pucker the front of your work. Weaving holds far better.

## The method: follow the stitches

Turn your piece to the wrong side and thread the tail onto a blunt tapestry needle. Then work a few stitches of **duplicate stitch**, also called Swiss darning: let the needle follow the path the yarn already takes through the fabric, so the tail sits snugly alongside it.

A few tips make it even more secure:

1. **Split the plies as you go.** Passing the needle through the yarn itself, rather than just under it, grips the tail and keeps it hidden from the front.
2. **Change direction.** Weave one way for several stitches, then make a U-turn and come back alongside. That little turn stops the tail from working loose.
3. **Weave a little more for slippery yarns.** Wool clings to itself, so fewer stitches will do. Cotton, linen, silk and bamboo need extra length and an extra turn or two.
4. **Keep it relaxed.** Don't pull the tail tight, or you'll lose the natural stretch and drape of the fabric.

## Choosing where to hide them

Garter ridges, ribbing columns, seams, picked-up edges and the backs of cables are all perfect hiding spots. Think about stretch, too: on a hat or a cuff that needs to stretch around, weave up and down rather than across so you don't stiffen the fabric.

## The finishing snip

Many knitters weave in ends, block the piece, and only then trim, leaving a short tail so it can settle in. Give the fabric a gentle stretch before you cut, then smooth it back into shape.
