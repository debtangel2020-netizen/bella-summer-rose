The festive season is nearly here, and a hand-knitted gift is one of the warmest things you can give. But many people who don't knit have never washed real wool by hand, and one hot machine cycle can shrink a favourite jumper for good. A small care note, tucked in with the wrapping, is a lovely way to help a gift last.

## What to put on the note

Keep it short and friendly, a few lines on a card or tag is plenty. These are the points that matter most:

- **Wash rarely.** Wool resists smells and stains, so airing it outside for a while between wears and dabbing small spills is often enough. A proper wash once or twice a season is usually all it needs.
- **Cool water, gentle soap.** Hand wash in cool water with a mild liquid made for wool or delicates. Skip fabric softener and washing powder.
- **Soak, don't scrub.** Let it soak for about ten to fifteen minutes and press gently. No rubbing hard, twisting or wringing.
- **Squeeze and roll.** Press out the water, then roll the piece in a towel to soak up the rest.
- **Dry flat.** Lay it on a dry towel, pat it back into shape and keep it out of direct sun, which can fade colours. Never hang a wet knit, as the weight stretches it.
- **Fold, don't hang.** Store knits folded. Before packing them away for summer, wash them and make sure they are completely dry, then keep them in a closed container to help keep moths away.
- **Little fluff balls are normal.** Wool can pill where it rubs. A fabric shaver or pilling comb tidies it up in minutes.

## If the yarn is machine washable

Some yarns, like superwash wool, can go in the machine on a cool wool or delicate cycle with a gentle spin. If you know the yarn, say so on the note. If you're not sure, hand washing is always the safe choice.

## Make it part of the gift

A care note doesn't have to feel like a set of rules. Write it in your own handwriting, add the yarn type if you know it, and tie it on with a bit of leftover yarn from the project. It tells the person you'd like them to wear it often and keep it for a long time, which is really the whole point of a handmade gift.
