A favourite jersey gets worn, and worn things wear thin. Elbows, collars and sock heels are usually the first to go. Instead of tucking the piece away, more and more knitters are choosing to mend it on purpose, in a colour that shows. It's called visible mending, and it's a lovely way to keep a handmade piece in use for years.

## Two simple ways to mend

**Swiss darning (also called duplicate stitch)** is a kind of embroidery worked on top of your knitting. With a blunt needle and a length of yarn, you trace the path of each existing knit stitch, so a new "V" sits right over the old one. Because it adds a second layer of yarn, it strengthens areas that are thinning but haven't broken through yet. It's also how people add little hearts, initials or stripes to plain knits.

**Woven darning** is for when there's an actual hole. You stretch a row of yarn across the gap from side to side, then weave a second yarn over and under those strands from top to bottom, like a tiny loom. Pushing the threads close together makes a firm little patch. Start and finish a few stitches beyond the hole so the edges can't keep unravelling.

## What you'll need

- A blunt darning (wool) needle, so you slip between stitches rather than splitting them.
- Yarn the same weight as the garment, or just a touch thicker.
- Something round to stretch the fabric over gently. A darning mushroom is traditional, but an orange works for socks, and a small embroidery hoop suits flatter pieces.

## A few gentle tips

1. **Catch it early.** Reinforcing a thin patch with Swiss darning is far easier than closing a hole later.
2. **Keep your tension relaxed.** Stitches pulled too tight will pucker the fabric. Each one should lie flat, with just enough yarn on top to cover the colour underneath.
3. **Play with colour.** A contrasting shade turns the mend into a feature. A bit of wobble is part of the charm, so there's no need for perfection.
4. **Tidy the back.** Weave your ends in on the wrong side, and take the shortest route between stitches to avoid bulk.

Mending is a quiet, cosy job for a summer evening, and it honours the hours that went into every handmade piece. Here at Bella Summer Rose, every item is knitted by hand, and we love the thought of them being cared for and worn for a long, long time.
