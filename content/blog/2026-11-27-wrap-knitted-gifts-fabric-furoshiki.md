A hand-knitted gift is soft, squishy and made to last, so it feels a little odd to wrap it in paper that gets torn off and thrown away a minute later. This year, why not try wrapping your knits in fabric instead? There's a lovely Japanese tradition for exactly this, called furoshiki.

## What is furoshiki?

Furoshiki is the art of wrapping things in a square of cloth, and the word is used for the cloth itself too. It goes back hundreds of years in Japan, where people first used these cloths at the public baths to carry their clothes and things. Over time they became an everyday way to carry food, belongings and, of course, presents.

The best part is that nothing goes to waste. A lot of shiny festive wrapping paper can't be recycled because of the dyes and coatings in it. A cloth can be used again and again, or given as part of the gift.

## Choosing your cloth

You don't need anything special. A tea towel, a cotton scarf, a bandana, a thin tablecloth or a square cut from a fabric remnant all work well. If you cut your own, hem the edges or trim them with pinking shears so they don't fray.

For size, aim for a square roughly three times as wide as the gift. Soft, folded knits like a beanie or a pair of socks are very forgiving, so don't worry about getting it exactly right.

## A simple wrap to start with

1. Lay the cloth flat, turned so it looks like a diamond.
2. Fold your knit neatly and place it in the centre.
3. Bring two opposite corners up over the gift and tie them in a firm knot.
4. Bring up the other two corners and tie them on top, either in a double knot or a pretty bow.
5. Tuck in a sprig of rosemary, a dried flower or a handwritten tag if you like.

It takes a few tries to feel natural, so practise on a jersey before the real thing. Children often love helping with this part too.
