Every knitter has one: a basket, a bag or a drawer full of little leftover bits of yarn that are too pretty to throw away but too short for a proper project. A magic yarn ball is a lovely way to give them a job. You join the scraps end to end, wind them into one ball, and knit with it as if it were a single yarn. The colours change all by themselves as you go, so every piece turns out a little different.

## Choosing your scraps

- **Keep the thickness similar.** Scraps that are close in weight give you an even fabric. Mixing a chunky yarn with a fine one tends to look lumpy.
- **Texture is welcome.** A smooth cotton next to a fuzzy or tweedy bit adds charm, as long as the weights roughly match.
- **Think about colour families.** Sorting scraps into groups, such as warm reds and pinks or soft neutrals, gives a calmer look. Tossing everything in together gives a joyful rainbow. Both are right.
- **Mind the length.** Very short pieces, around 20 cm or less, are fiddly to join, so save those for stuffing toys or tying gift tags.

## Joining without the fuss

The point of a magic ball is to avoid a mountain of ends at the finish, so the join matters. The **magic knot** is the favourite: you tie each yarn around the other so the two knots slide together and lock, then trim the tails close. It is quick and secure, though it can leave a tiny bump. A **Russian join**, where each tail is threaded back through its own strand, takes a little longer but leaves no knot at all, which is handy where you want a clean change of colour. Try a couple of joins on spare bits first, because some fibres grip better than others.

As you wind, keep turning the ball and changing the angle of your wraps so it stays nice and round.

## What to knit

Magic balls shine in projects where a surprise sequence of colours is part of the fun: a stripy hat, a scarf, a granny-square blanket worked a square at a time, or small useful things like coasters, cosies and dishcloths. Little scrappy makes also make sweet last-minute gifts for the festive season.

It is a gentle, thrifty way to make room in your stash and end up with something one of a kind, which is exactly how we feel about every piece we knit by hand here at Bella Summer Rose.

Happy winding!
