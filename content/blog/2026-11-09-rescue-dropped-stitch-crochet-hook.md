Every knitter meets a dropped stitch sooner or later. One moment your rows look lovely, the next there's a loose loop and a little ladder running down the fabric. Happily, you rarely need to unravel anything. With a crochet hook and a bit of patience, you can climb that stitch back up to where it belongs.

## First, stop the run

Before anything else, catch the loose loop so it can't slip any further. A safety pin, a locking stitch marker or even a spare needle threaded through it will do. If more than one stitch has escaped, secure each of them the same way and fix them one at a time.

Choose a crochet hook about the same size as your knitting needles, or a little smaller. A hook that's too big will stretch the stitches.

## Climbing back up the ladder

Look just above your loose loop and you'll see a set of horizontal strands, like the rungs of a ladder. Each rung is one row of yarn that the stitch missed. These steps are for stocking stitch, worked with the smooth knit side facing you:

1. Slip the hook into the loose loop from the front.
2. Catch the lowest rung and draw it through the loop on your hook. That's one row repaired.
3. Carry on with the next rung up, and the next, always working them in order from the bottom.
4. When no rungs are left, place the loop back on your left needle without twisting it, and knit on as normal.

If you grab the wrong rung and things look bunched, simply let the loop drop back down and start again.

## Purl side and patterns

For a purl stitch, the easiest trick is to turn the work so the knit side faces you, then follow the same steps. In garter stitch, the rows alternate, so you'll turn the work (or change the direction you pull the strand through) for each rung.

## Smoothing it out

The repaired stitch may look a touch tighter or looser than its neighbours. Give the fabric a gentle tug sideways and lengthways to share out the yarn, and remember that washing and blocking evens out most small differences.

If you only spot the ladder many rows later, it's still worth trying. If it won't settle, unpicking back to the dropped stitch is a perfectly good plan too.

Be kind to yourself: dropped stitches happen to everyone, even on the pieces we knit by hand here at Bella Summer Rose.
